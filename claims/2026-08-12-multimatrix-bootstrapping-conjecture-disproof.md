## The claim
A blogger writing as "Casual Physics Enjoyer" — who studied theoretical
physics to Master's level at Cambridge (a 98/100 final-year score, per their
own account) but is not a professional research mathematician — says they
used **GPT Sol Ultra** first to search for open conjectures they could
personally understand and verify, and then to generate a **disproof** of one
of them.

The target is **Conjecture 2** from Khalkhali, Pagliaroli, Parfeni & Smith,
["Bootstrapping the critical behavior of multi-matrix
models"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07565) (JHEP 02 (2025) 158). For the
2-matrix model with configuration (g, α, β) = (g, g, g), the conjecture
proposes that near a critical point g_c the free energy derivative behaves as
d/dg F₀ ∼ (1 − g/g_c)^(2 − 2·(1/2)), i.e. that the **string susceptibility
exponent is γ = 1/2** — which would place the model's continuum limit in the
universality class of the Continuum Random Tree.

Specifics:
- The author reports GPT Sol Ultra produced a full argument that this
  exponent claim is **false**, with the working shown as an image in a
  linked ChatGPT conversation (not transcribed as text in the post itself).
- The author is explicit about their own uncertainty: *"I am well aware of
  being wrong,"* and says they used ordinary algebra tools rather than a
  formal verifier (no Lean/Coq check).
- They have **emailed the original authors** (Khalkhali, Pagliaroli,
  Parfeni, Smith) asking them to check the proof, but no response was
  reported as of the post.

## Why it needs watching
- **Outsider profile fits well** — the author explicitly used AI to pick a
  problem within reach and then to do the actual disproof, rather than using
  AI as an assistive tool within their own established expertise.
- **Self-reported and self-verified only** — no independent check, no
  response yet from the paper's authors, and the proof itself isn't even
  fully transcribed as text (image of a chat log). This is much thinner
  evidence than the other AI-math claims already tracked here.
- The author's own stated uncertainty is a good sign of intellectual
  honesty, but also means this could evaporate quickly once someone with
