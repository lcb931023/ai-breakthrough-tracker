## The claim
Mathematicians Philip Arathoon (Babson College), Gavin Ball (University of
Missouri), and Matthew D. Kvalheim (UMBC) posted **"The Maxwell Conjecture is
False"** to arXiv on 29 July 2026, disproving the **Maxwell conjecture**: a
1873-treatise-inspired bound formulated by Gabrielov, Novikov, and Shapiro
(2007) stating that if the critical points (equilibria) of the electrostatic
potential generated by **n** point charges are all non-degenerate, their
number cannot exceed **(n − 1)²**.

Specifics:
- Counterexample: **5 point charges** — three unit charges at the vertices of
  an equilateral triangle, plus two small charges q_ε placed symmetrically
  along the perpendicular axis at ±ε, with q_ε = (3/4)ε³ − (5/32)ε⁵. For
  sufficiently small ε this configuration has **at least 24 non-degenerate
  critical points** — Maxwell's bound for n = 5 would cap it at (5 − 1)² = 16.
- The paper's explicit **"Tool and computational resource disclosure"**
  section states: *"The idea behind this construction was suggested by an
  LLM (OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol). The authors have verified the mathematical
  details and have written the argument in their own words. Computer algebra
  software (Mathematica, Maple) was used to verify computations and produce
  visualisations."*
- The construction is also shown to generalize (Proposition 1): iterating it
  gives configurations of 3 + 2m charges with at least 4 + 20m non-degenerate
  critical points, an asymptotic critical-point-to-charge ratio of 10 —
  better than the previous best of 25/7 from Edelsbrunner, Fillmore, and
  Oliveira (2026).
- Proof is a fully worked classical-analysis argument (Taylor expansion,
  implicit function theorem, parametric transversality), not a black-box
  assertion — independently checkable by any mathematician willing to follow
  the algebra.

Reported via [arXiv:2607.27197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27197)
(physics.class-ph), posted 29 July 2026.

## Why it needs watching
- **Not the outsider profile** — three credentialed mathematicians using AI
  as a research tool, not an outsider using AI to enter a field cold. The
  AI-engine test still passes cleanly, though: the paper's own disclosure
  credits the LLM specifically with the generative step (the triangular
  bipyramid + shrinking axial-charges idea), distinct from the humans'
  verification and write-up work.
- **Preprint only** — not yet peer-reviewed, and no independent replication
  or third-party mathematician commentary found as of logging.
- The mathematics is concrete and checkable (explicit polynomial expansions,
  numerically illustrated in the paper's figures), so this is a good
  candidate for relatively fast community verification.
- Track: does a specialist in electrostatics/critical-point-counting (e.g.
  authors of the works it builds on — Gabrielov, Novikov, Shapiro,
  Edelsbrunner, Zolotov) confirm the construction? Does it clear peer
  review? Does any commentary dispute how much credit belongs to GPT-5.6 Sol
  versus the authors' own derivation?
